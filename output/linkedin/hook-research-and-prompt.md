# Hook cover research and prompt

Created using the built-in image generation tool.

## Research

- LinkedIn recommends a clear CTA and testing different creative messages: https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/best-practices/ad-tips/sponsored-content-tips
- Nielsen Norman Group describes size, contrast and space as tools for directing visual attention: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/

These inform the design; they do not establish that this particular cover will improve organic LinkedIn engagement. LinkedIn's cited guidance concerns sponsored content.

## Design choice

Lead with a relatable question, then show one concrete chat-to-approval example. Place before the four explanatory images. No engagement or savings claims.

## Final prompt

Create a premium square 1:1 LinkedIn carousel OPENING COVER for a developer's real Expense Assistant project. This must hook attention quickly with a single bold idea, not an information-dense technical infographic. Editorial product campaign aesthetic, warm ivory background, deep forest green lettering, emerald highlights matching existing Expense Assistant graphics. Exceptionally clean typography and generous space, polished subtle 3D depth only in the central UI illustration. Top small label 'EXPENSE ASSISTANT'. Main headline in very large crisp bold sans-serif, exact text split into 3 readable lines: 'What if tracking' / 'expenses felt' / 'like texting?' Make 'like texting?' emerald. Below headline, one visually compelling large chat bubble containing exact text 'Spent ₹250 on lunch'. Connect with a delicate arrow to a simple beautiful approval card, text 'Review expense', row 'Lunch' and '₹250', and two buttons 'Approve' and 'Reject'. Small caption beneath this card 'Illustrative workflow'. A small supporting sentence near bottom: 'Describe it. Review it. Record it.' Bottom CTA 'Explore how it works →'. Discreet bottom technology line 'FastAPI · LangChain · PostgreSQL'. Do not add any other text. No slide numbering, robot mascots, charts, fake metrics, bank logos, claims of guaranteed savings or production readiness. Hierarchy must prioritize headline then the chat example then the call to action. Composition should read instantly on a mobile feed, with all text safely inset from edges. Make it feel distinctive and professionally art-directed, not a generic template.
